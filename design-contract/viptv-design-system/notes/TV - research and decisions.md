# TV — research and decisions

What the big TV apps do, why, and what I took:

1. Top text tabs (Netflix 2025, Disney+ 2025, Hulu). Words are faster to read than icons from the couch, and a top bar costs no width, so rows get the full screen. It hides once you move into rows.

2. The art is the headline (all of them). Title logos instead of typed titles; one quiet UI typeface (Onest) so the posters carry the personality. Bricolage is kept for the wordmark only.

3. First row peeks above the fold (Netflix shrank its billboard so you see what you were watching without scrolling).

4. The top of the screen describes the focused title while you browse (Netflix, Prime Video, Max). You can decide without opening a page — Netflix calls the goal fighting decision fatigue.

5. Focused button = solid white, others translucent (Apple TV). Primary is wide with a label; secondary actions are round icon buttons. No accent buttons.

6. Decision badges on art: New episode, New, Watched (Netflix, Disney+). Format and rating badges in the meta line: 4K, HDR, 5.1, PG-13.

7. One scrolling title page: actions, then seasons and episodes with synopses, then More Like This (Apple TV, Disney+). Resume shows its progress inside the button.

8. Calm geometry: 10px cards, 12px buttons, round icon buttons; a thin white inner frame for focus; nothing scales.

9. Accent only for progress and the logo mark.

10. No key hints anywhere. Fire TV and Android TV say focus must always be obvious; if focus is obvious, OK doesn't need a label.

11. Sign-in, every method: phone (QR), browser + code, email code, password, IPTV provider (Xtream / M3U), or no account. Phone first because typing with a remote is slow (Google device sign-in guide; FX Digital). Greet by name, then continue on its own.

12. Search: alphabetical 6-column grid keyboard (shortest D-pad paths), results update per letter, channels included.

13. Live: channels down, time across, a red now-line, the focused programme described above with a still of the channel (nothing autoplays).

14. Sources: Play already picks the best match, so the panel is an override; it fills in as add-ons answer instead of blocking.

15. Player (Netflix 2025 test, tvOS 15): no on-screen play/pause (the remote has them); one cluster above a bigger scrubber; audio, subtitles and subtitle size in one sheet you change without pausing; Skip intro appears only when relevant; credits shrink to the corner and a Play next episode button waits — nothing ever plays on its own.

16. Errors offer the next best source first.

17. Motion board lists every animation with timings; the Home → Continue Watching transition is its own looping board. All motion respects reduced motion.
