# TV UI review — Motion, hero, loading and the skip affordance

This note records the design reasoning behind the `feature/tv-ui-polish` work:
what was wrong, what each change is modelled on, and which numbers are ours.
It is not a hardware qualification; see `TESTING.md` and
`tests/PARITY_MATRIX.md` for the evidence split.

## 1. Why it felt janky

Seven causes, each fixed by a structural change rather than a timing tweak.

| Cause | What it looked like | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawers animated by timers | The source side-sheet popped in and out; a slow frame left it half-way | `SlidingDrawer` runs the slide on the renderer's clock and reports `settled`/`closed` as **events**. Nothing closes on a `setTimeout`. |
| The scene resized while a drawer was open | Every node re-laid out, then re-laid out again on close: two full-scene passes per open/close | The scene keeps its full size and is dimmed (`sceneWidth()` is gone). One constant composition. |
| Spinners and progress bars on `setInterval` | Visible stutter, and an interval that fought its own transition | Animation runs on the renderer (`animateProp`), which advances with the frame clock. `DetailSkeleton` (a 1100 ms interval under a 1000 ms transition) is deleted outright. |
| Animation settings rebuilt per render | Every render restarted the run from zero (settings compare **by identity**) | All settings objects are module-level constants. |
| A cold playback engine | Pressing Play paid for engine setup before the first frame | `DeferredPlayer.prewarm()` runs when the source drawer opens and once after Home's first paint (`whenIdle()`), never between the press and the frame. |
| Two different busy geometries | The preparation spinner and the seek indicator sat at different heights and jumped between states | One `BusySurface` at one rect (`800,476,320,132`, spinner `56×56` at `932,476`). |
| Hero artwork cut by a baked scrim | The artwork stopped in a hard-edged panel; the mask assumed one layout | The hero is an ambient band that ends on the carousel line and fades into the page ground (below). |

The general rule we took from this: **an animation ends where the renderer says
it ended, not where a timer says it should have.** The drawer, the busy ring,
the hero bar and the splash all follow it.

## 2. The hero: an ambient band, not a panel

The old hero was a sharp 1440×810 artwork panel beside the title, clipped at
480–1920, with a full-screen scrim baked for exactly that geometry. It read as a
photograph glued to the corner: an abrupt bottom edge, a dead black left column,
and a mask that could not follow any other layout.

The hero is now a **blurred band across the top of the frame** that ends exactly
where the poster carousel begins and dissolves into the page ground over its
bottom 35%.

- **Where it ends** is not a constant: the carousel starts at 824 in hero mode,
  690.4 on the Continue row and 545.2 on a poster row. `hero-stage.ts` owns those
  numbers and `App` reads `CAROUSEL_TOP` from the same module, so the band and
  the shelves cannot drift apart. A geometry change moves both.
- **Blur** is a server-side proxy request (`blur=60`, decoded 1280 wide) in every
  band mode, not just the portrait row. This is Apple TV's ambient treatment:
  content behind text should be colour and texture, not a competing photograph.
  It also keeps the TV CPU out of it — no runtime blur, no readback.
- **The dissolve** is one baked texture: a horizontal wash (0.9 at the left edge,
  clear by 1440) times a vertical ramp over the bottom 35%. The texture is
  stretched over the band, so one asset serves every band height and every
  screen resolution. It is generated by `scripts/build-band-scrim.mjs` with
  `node:zlib`.
- **Legibility bound**: the wash keeps the whole synopsis column at most ~30% of
  the artwork's brightness over pure white (`hero-layering.spec.ts` asserts both
  the far-left column and its right edge). The previous design kept that column
  completely black; the ambient band deliberately spends a little of that
  contrast on the effect and no more.
- **No snap when the band changes height**: the scrim belongs to the *frame*, so
  it crossfades with the artwork. A shared scrim would have jumped 280 px while
  the two artworks dissolved into each other.

## 3. Loading: show the ground, arrive once

The detail screen used to draw a skeleton of bars and rectangles that pulsed
forever and rarely matched the real layout, then swapped to the content. It was
removed. While a title loads, the screen is the page ground; when the answer and
its prepared artwork agree, the body arrives as **one 200 ms decelerating fade**
(`motion.arrive()`). Nothing is invented in between, so nothing has to be taken
back.

## 4. Skip intro

What the TV apps we are matching do:

- **Netflix**: one `Skip Intro` button appears on its own during the title
  sequence, in the lower right, without the viewer opening the controls. It is
  offered for a short window and then withdraws; the action stays reachable from
  the player controls, and the button is gone once the segment ends. A recap gets
  `Skip recap`.
- **Apple TV (tvOS)**: the Siri Remote's **Play/Pause** button doubles as
  "skip the intro" while the skip offer is on screen. A dedicated pause key still
  pauses. (Apple's HIG treats Play/Pause as the primary transport key.)

What we implemented:

| Behaviour | Ours |
| --- | --- |
| One button, one place | `SKIP_BUTTON = {1596, 846, 228, 56}` — the far right of the control row, where Next episode also lives. The standalone button and the row button are the same component at the same rect. |
| Offered for a while, then withdrawn | 6 s (`SKIP_OFFER_MS`), then a 200 ms fade (`SKIP_FADE_MS`). The numbers are ours; the behaviour is Netflix's. |
| Still reachable afterwards | Skip is the control row's last button, so it keeps a normal focus ring and is one D-pad step away once the controls are open. |
| Gone with the segment | The active segment is derived from position and the server's markers; the button unmounts when the segment ends. Invalid markers (negative, empty, beyond the duration) and `credits` never offer it. |
| Play/Pause skips | `input/player-media.ts` seeks past the segment for `MediaPlayPause`/`MediaPlay` while a segment is current. `MediaPause` (key 19) still pauses, and Space keeps its browser meaning. |
| One seek path | `skipSegment()` is the only implementation; Select, the row button and the remote key all call it, so the paused intent, the lease seek and the chrome restore cannot diverge. |

Previously the skip slot silently replaced the **Play** control, so pressing OK
on Play skipped the intro, and the standalone button never went away. Both are
gone.

## 5. Layout

Every screen now reads its edges from `src/tv-solid/remote/layout.ts`. The
values are unchanged — this is naming, not redesign — which is what lets
`layout-audit.spec.ts` assert a *cross-screen* grid: no screen draws text left of
the inset, Home/Library/Live anchor on it, and the player keeps its control row
at 846 and its timeline at 936 with the content width.

The frame is a fixed 1920×1080 logical space and the renderer scales it, so
these are resolution-independent by construction: a 4K panel and a 720p panel
lay out identically and only the raster differs. No component invents a
resolution-specific number.
